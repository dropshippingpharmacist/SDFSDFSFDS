The ten ideas he repeats in almost every video

1. High ticket only. Fewer, better clients at $3K–$6K+ beat hundreds of $100 subscribers.
2. The business is a chain of rates. Views → visits → bookings → shows → closes → cash. Find the weakest link, fix it, repeat.
3. YouTube sells. Instagram keeps you in mind. Short form brings strangers; long form turns them into buyers.
4. Never sell to cold people. Cold → warm → hot, with top, middle and bottom-of-funnel content.
5. Hook → retention → reward. The structure behind every video, story sequence and sales video.
6. Each step only sells the next step. The first video sells the click, not the program.
7. Organic first. No ads until at least $100K a month. "Organic is the fire, ads are the fuel."
8. Close your own calls first. No sales team until about $25K–$30K a month.
9. Delivery is the flywheel. Student results become testimonials, which become content, which brings students.
10. Track everything. "You cannot fix what you don't know." Decide with 50+ data points, not feelings.

Module 2

Why high ticket: the math

His strongest opinion. The same 100 viewers are worth roughly 20 times more with a call-based offer.

Same 100 warm viewers, two business models (his numbers)

What he says goes wrong with low ticket

  • Members stay 2–3 months, so lifetime value is only $200–$250.
  • 30–40% leave every month. You are always replacing people.
  • More refunds and disputes, which can get a payment account frozen.
  • You feel forced to post trades daily to keep people subscribed.
  • Usually caps at $10K–$20K a month.

What he says high ticket gives you

  • $10K a month is 3–5 clients, about one sale a week.
  • Buyers have paid enough to do the work, so results are better.
  • Better results mean testimonials, which mean more buyers.
  • Margins of 80%+ with almost no team.
  • Each sale can grow: upgrades, renewals, payment plans.
What he teaches

"People don't buy into trading signal groups. They buy into people." He calls signal groups "outdated and saturated" and tells signal sellers to build a personal brand with a mentorship instead.

Your version

This matches the decision you already made: sell the mentorship, downsell the course, and include the signals instead of selling them. Your bot stops being a $50 product and becomes the reason your mentorship is different from everyone else's.

Careful

His model assumes your delivery really is worth $5,000. The price only holds if students get a real change, so Module 11 matters as much as the selling.

Payment plans cost more than paying in full

Mentorship$5,0002 × $3,000

Pay-in-full clearance: anyone on a plan can clear the balance at the pay-in-full price within 30 days. Example: paid $3,000, clears with $2,000 instead of another $3,000. He says this lifts cash up front and plan completion.

His three pricing tests
  1. Your hourly rate. $5,000 ÷ about 15 hours of your time = $333/hour. If your business as a whole earns more per hour than that, raise the price.
  2. Student return. The program should be able to make them about 10× the price over time.
  3. Your close rate. Closing over 60–80% of calls means you're too cheap. Under 20–30% means too expensive or the wrong leads.
What he teaches
  • Start with 1-on-1 as the main offer. It's the easiest to sell and gives the best results and testimonials.
  • In one case study the split was 65% main offer, 25% downsell, 10% upsell. Without the other two offers, 35% of buyers would have been lost.
  • Never sell lifetime coaching or lifetime community. A course's videos can be lifetime, because they cost you no time.
  • Use contracts, signed automatically, for every sale.
Your version
  • Mentorship, 90 days: a call every two weeks, direct chat with you, the full curriculum, signals for all markets, the dashboard. Optional BloFin auto-trade after the risk module.
  • Course: 8–12 recorded lessons plus 60 days of signals. Record it in weeks 2–3. Until it exists, the downsell is "join the waitlist".
  • Lifetime signals: his "never offer lifetime" rule is why I suggested capping seats and wording it "for the life of the service". Monthly continuation is the safer default.
Careful

He promises results on his pages ("guaranteed") with $1,000 back. Don't guarantee trading profits; in most places that is a legal problem for anything investment-related. Offer a guarantee on what you control: "do the work for 14 days; if it isn't what I described, full refund."

Your version: the short-form series

His best-performing short format is a numbered daily series ("Day 8 of…"). Viewers see a number, go back to Day 1 and binge. Yours writes itself: "Day N: my bot's trades today" or "Road to 100 public trades", 30–45 seconds each: what it took, what it skipped, the running result. Wins and losses.

More ideas from his list: batch 3–4 talking videos on the weekend; schedule everything so daily posting isn't a chore; make the content your past self needed; ask every student what they struggle with and film the answers; when one video takes off, make more on that topic.

What he teaches about proof and losses

A raw 52-minute video of a top creator losing $34,000 made more sales than a "new car" lifestyle video. Lifestyle attracts beginners; honest trading attracts buyers. "Never think you have a boring life," but keep YouTube educational.

Who should be talking
Record every call (he uses Fathom) and check the talk ratio. That client turned the training into a one-page checklist and followed it on each call.
The three outcomes on the call
Fits and can invest → Mentorship, pay in full or 2 payments
Fits, truly can't afford it → Course, on the call
Doesn't fit → say so, send free content
He prefers taking payment on the call, with contracts signed automatically after.
How he says to raise the close rate
  • Match their pain. Use the application answers and the pre-call video they watched.
  • Pre-handle objections in public. If "will this work with a small account?" keeps coming up, answer it in a Q&A story, the after-booking page and a video.
  • Ask every new student why they bought. A 15-question form after purchase: what made you buy, the tipping point, what almost stopped you. After 50 answers you know exactly what to say in content.
  • Don't hire friends or students to sell. And not before the offer is selling well with you on the calls.
Your version: the objections you'll hear
They sayWhere you answer it before the call
"Is the bot's record long enough?"Public results page; the 30-day video; your own documented history.
"I only have a small account."The sizing video: risk is a percentage, not a dollar figure.
"Why not just buy the signals?"Story sequence: signals without sizing and discipline still lose money. The mentorship is the sizing and discipline.
"I need to think about it."The 14-day refund promise and the pay-in-full clearance window.
Careful

Sell with their real situation, not pressure. If someone would be putting in money they can't afford to lose, the right answer is the free content, even if they want to buy. That protects them and your reputation.

What he teaches
  • A waitlist turned into 330 booked calls in 24 hours for one client with about 4,200 on the list.
  • Waitlist leads book first, and close better.
  • Send emails at the time your audience is most active (his client's was 12:30).
  • Seasonal angles work. He calls New Year "a gold mine": "don't waste another year."
  • A pre-launch needs an existing audience. With 10 followers, build first.
Your version
  • Have the page, application, booking link and after-booking video ready before day 1.
  • Open a real, limited number: for example 8 mentorship spots.
  • It's mid-November now. A build-up through December with a January opening fits his New Year angle exactly.
  • Expect a few sales from a first launch, not hundreds. The goal is your first 4–8 students and their results.

What I would not copy

His system works because of the structure, not because of these tactics. Leaving them out costs little and protects you.

Guaranteed results

"Become a six-figure trader, guaranteed." Promising trading outcomes invites legal trouble and refunds. Guarantee your delivery, not their profits.

Fake scarcity

A spots counter coded to drop by itself, and "reply YES or your call is cancelled" when he admits it won't be. Use real limits; you have them.

Pretending to be you

A setter writing as "the coach", or a video faked to look personally recorded. Have helpers say they're on your team.

Blocking whole countries

He auto-rejects by phone prefix. The budget question already does the job fairly, person by person.

Income claims

Show your real numbers with context and "results not typical". Never suggest a student will repeat $7K to $1.1M.

His numbers

Every revenue figure and benchmark in this course is self-reported by someone selling a service. Treat them as targets to test, not promises.
