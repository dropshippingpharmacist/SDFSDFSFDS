# Video: "What I'd do with $1,000 today if I was starting over"

About 8–10 minutes.

---

**[FACE] HOOK (0:00–0:15)**

If I lost everything and had to start again with one thousand dollars, I would not try to turn it into a million fast. Here's exactly what I'd do instead, step by step.

**[FACE] STEP 1: PROTECT IT (0:15–2:00)**

First rule: a thousand dollars is not a lottery ticket. If you lose it, you're out of the game. Staying in the game is the first job.

So I'd risk a tiny fixed amount per trade. [E.g. "one percent, ten dollars."] That sounds boring. It means I could be wrong twenty times in a row and still have most of the account.

**[FACE] STEP 2: ONE MARKET, ONE METHOD (2:00–3:30)**

I'd pick one or two major markets, not fifty random coins. And one method I understand, with clear rules for when to enter and when I'm wrong.

**[CHART] STEP 3: ONLY TRADES THAT PAY MORE THAN THEY RISK (3:30–5:00)**

Every trade needs a target at least [two or three] times bigger than the risk. Show the math again: risk 10 to make 30. Lose six trades out of ten and still be up.

**[FACE] STEP 4: TRADE LESS (5:00–6:30)**

I'd take far fewer trades. A handful of good ones a month beats a handful of random ones a day. That's exactly how my bot works: it skips almost everything.

**[FACE] STEP 5: TRACK EVERYTHING (6:30–7:30)**

Every trade goes in a journal: entry, stop, result, and why I took it. After fifty trades you know whether you actually have an edge, instead of guessing.

**[FACE] STEP 6: ADD MONEY, NOT RISK (7:30–8:15)**

As the account grows, I'd add savings to it, not increase the risk per trade. Growth comes from consistency and time.
