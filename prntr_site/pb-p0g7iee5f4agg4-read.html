Part 0

Start today: your first hour

You don't need to read everything before you start. Do these steps today, in this order, and you'll have placed your first trade the right way: small, planned, and protected.

  1. Pick your test money (5 min). An amount you could lose completely without it changing your week. Write the number down. That's your starting bankroll, and you don't add to it this month.
  2. Set up the burner wallet (15 min). Install one wallet app in a separate browser profile, write the seed phrase on paper, and send only your test money to it. (Part 2 has the full security list for later.)
  3. Find a coin from a wallet that keeps winning (15 min). Open a wallet tracker, pick one wallet that passes the six filters in Part 5, and look at its newest buy.
  4. Run the 12-point rug check on it (5 min). If it fails any of the first five checks, skip it and go back to step 3. Skipping is a win.
  5. Fill in the trade plan card (5 min). Your amount (a small slice of the test money), where you take profit (the ladder in Part 6) and the point where you'll accept it's not working.
  6. Place the buy, then set alerts (5 min). Price alerts at 2x and 5x so the plan runs even when you're not watching.
  7. Journal it (5 min). Write down why you took it. Win or lose, this is the trade you learn the most from.
Leverage comes after a week of this, not today. When you do start, use the calculator below at the smallest size your exchange allows. The goal of week one is to follow the rules every time, not to get rich. That part comes from repetition.
Part 1

The money loop: two engines and a safe stack

Most people use one tool for everything. That's why they blow up. I split my money into three places, and every dollar has one job.

Engine 1 · MemecoinsGrowth. Small bets with huge upside, copied from wallets that keep winning. Most go to zero; a few pay for everything.
Engine 2 · LeverageCash flow. Fixed risk on every trade, only when the setup confirms. Boring on purpose.
Safe stackWhat you keep. Stablecoins, Bitcoin, cash in the bank. Off the trading accounts.

The rule that makes it work: money only moves out of the riskiest place, never back in to "win it back". Memecoin wins fund the leverage account and the safe stack. Leverage profits pay you. Losses never get refilled from your safe stack.

Part 2

Lock down your setup before you make a single trade

More people lose crypto to hacks and phishing than to bad trades. This part takes an hour and protects everything after it.

Use three wallets, not one

WalletWhat it's forRule
BurnerBuying memecoins, connecting to new sitesOnly holds what you're trading this week. Assume it will get drained one day.
MainReceiving profits, moving money between placesNever connects to a new site or signs anything you don't understand.
VaultYour safe stackA hardware wallet. It almost never moves.

The non-negotiables

  • Your seed phrase lives on paper or metal, offline. Never in a photo, a note app, an email or the cloud.
  • Nobody legit will ever ask for your seed phrase. Not support, not a "dev", not an airdrop. Ever.
  • Use a separate browser profile just for crypto, with only your wallet extension installed.
  • Bookmark the sites you use. Never click links from DMs, replies or search ads: fake sites copy real ones perfectly.
  • Read what you're signing. "Approve unlimited spending" or "set approval for all" on a site you don't know means "steal everything".
  • Every month, review and revoke old token approvals with an approval checker (for example revoke.cash on EVM chains).
  • App-based 2FA (not SMS) and a unique password on every exchange account. Turn on withdrawal whitelists.
  • Never keep more on an exchange than you're actively trading.
Part 3

How memecoins actually move

Memecoins don't trade on value. They trade on attention, and attention moves in a cycle. Know where you are in it.

PhaseWhat it looks likeWho's buying
1 · LaunchTiny market cap, chaotic chart, bots everywhereSnipers, insiders, the dev's friends
2 · DiscoveryHolders growing, first real community, early callsSmart wallets and early buyers
3 · HypeTrending everywhere, influencers posting, big green candlesEveryone. This is where the early money sells.
4 · DistributionPrice stalls on huge volume, wicks on both sidesLate buyers absorbing the sellers
5 · BleedLower highs, dead socials, "it'll come back"Bag holders
The money is made by getting in during discovery and selling into the hype. If you only hear about a coin once it's trending everywhere, you're usually the exit liquidity.
Part 4

The 12-point rug check (run it before every buy)

Free tools do most of the work: a token scanner (RugCheck on Solana, Token Sniffer or GoPlus on EVM chains), a chart site like DexScreener or Birdeye, a block explorer, and a holder map like Bubblemaps. Two minutes per coin. If it fails one of the first five, walk away.

  1. Mint authority revoked. If the dev can still print new tokens, they can dump on you any time.
  2. Freeze authority revoked (Solana). Otherwise the dev can freeze your tokens so you can't sell.
  3. You can actually sell. Scanners flag honeypots and high sell taxes. Never assume.
  4. Liquidity burned or locked. Unlocked liquidity can be pulled in one transaction.
  5. Top 10 holders own a small share (excluding the liquidity pool). One wallet with 20% can end the chart.
  6. No bundled launch. Many wallets buying in the same block, funded from one source, is the dev hiding their supply.
  7. The dev wallet's history. Check what they did with their last coins. Serial ruggers repeat.
  8. Holder clusters. A holder map shows groups of wallets linked to each other. Big clusters = one person.
  9. Liquidity vs your size. If your buy moves the price a lot, your sell will too. Your exit matters more than your entry.
  10. Real volume. Thousands of tiny same-size trades are bots faking activity.
  11. Socials with history. An account made yesterday with bought followers is a warning, not a community.
  12. The story spreads by itself. Real attention comes from people who weren't paid to post.

The rug check keeps you out of the worst coins. The wallets I actually follow, and how I find new ones early, are what I share 1-on-1.

Part 5

Copy trading: how to judge a wallet before you follow it

Most of my memecoin money came from following wallets that keep winning. Wallet trackers (GMGN, Cielo and similar) show any wallet's trades and results. The hard part is telling skill from luck. Use these filters:

  • Many trades, not one hit. A wallet with one 100x and 200 losses isn't a trader to follow.
  • Profit across months, not just this week's mania.
  • It sells. Good wallets take profit into strength. Wallets that only buy are often bots or insiders.
  • Normal sizes. If they buy with sizes you could never match, their trades won't work the same for you.
  • Not the dev, not a sniper. Wallets that are always first in the same block are bots or insiders. You can't copy that timing.
  • Their entries leave room for you. If the price is already 5x past their buy by the time you see it, you're not copying them, you're chasing them.
Copy the decision, not blindly the size. Your size is set by your own rules (next part), never by theirs.
Part 6

Sizing and taking profit on memecoins

Size every buy as if it goes to zero

Because many will. Set one fixed amount per coin, a small slice of your memecoin money, and keep it the same whether you "feel good" or not. A rule many traders use: no single coin over 1–2% of your total crypto.

A simple profit ladder

When it hitsDo thisWhy
2xSell your starting amountNow you're playing with house money. Nothing left to lose.
5xSell another chunkLock in a real win before the hype cools.
10x and upKeep selling into big green candlesSell into strength, never into a crash.
AlwaysLeave a small "moonbag"So you never regret selling the one that runs 100x.

An example ladder, not a rule. The point is to decide your exits before you buy.

The mistake that costs the most: watching a 10x turn back into a 1x because you were waiting for 20x. Profit isn't real until it's sold.
Part 7

Leverage without liquidation

Leverage doesn't make you richer. It makes you use less money to hold the same position. That's the whole point, and the whole danger.

The words you need

  • Margin: the money you put up. Position size: margin × leverage.
  • Liquidation: the price where the exchange closes you because your margin is gone.
  • Isolated margin: only that trade's margin can be lost. Cross margin: your whole account backs every trade. Beginners: isolated.
  • Funding: a small fee paid between longs and shorts every few hours. It adds up on trades you hold for days.

How close is liquidation? (isolated, roughly)

LeveragePrice move that liquidates you
5xabout 20%
10xabout 10%
20xabout 5%
50xabout 2%
100xabout 1%

Roughly 100% ÷ leverage, a bit less after fees and the exchange's maintenance margin. Your exchange shows the exact price before you confirm.

The rule: your stop loss must always be hit long before your liquidation price. If it isn't, your position is too big or your leverage is too high. A liquidation means you skipped this rule.
Part 8

Position size in one line

This is the most important formula in trading, and almost nobody uses it. You decide how much you're willing to lose first, then the trade size comes out of it.

Position size = money you'll risk ÷ distance to your stop (as a %)

Worked example

  • Account: $10,000. Risk per trade: 1% = $100.
  • Your stop is 2% away from your entry.
  • Position size = $100 ÷ 2% = $5,000.
  • At 10x, that only needs $500 of margin, and liquidation is about 10% away, far past your 2% stop.
  • If the stop hits, you lose $100. Not your account. $100.
Notice what decided the size: your risk and your stop. The leverage only decided how much margin you tie up. That's how professionals use leverage.
Part 9

The math that pays you: R

"R" is what you risk on one trade. If you risk $100 and make $300, that's a 3R win. Thinking in R changes everything, because it tells you how often you actually need to be right.

If you win…you only need to be right10 trades at that win rate
1R (1 to 1)more than 50% of the time5 wins, 5 losses = 0R
2Rmore than 33%4 wins (+8R), 6 losses (−6R) = +2R
3Rmore than 25%4 wins (+12R), 6 losses (−6R) = +6R
4Rmore than 20%3 wins (+12R), 7 losses (−7R) = +5R
With trades that pay 3 to 1, you can lose 6 out of 10 and still be up. That's why the bot skips anything that doesn't pay several times its risk.

Part 10

The 7-point checklist my bot runs before it risks a dollar

My bot can't trade on a feeling. Every trade has to pass these seven checks. If one fails, it waits.

  1. Patience first. Most of the time the right trade is no trade. It waits for the market to show its hand.
  2. A clear story on the chart. Levels the market has respected more than once. A messy chart is a skip.
  3. Know who's trapped. Every move needs someone on the wrong side. Trade with the side in control.
  4. A level with history. Areas where serious buying or selling showed up before, never a random line.
  5. Confirmation, not prediction. It waits for the market to prove the idea, then gets in.
  6. The math has to work. It knows where it's wrong and where it takes profit before it enters, and the reward has to be several times the risk.
  7. Discipline. Same risk every trade, no chasing a move that already left, no fighting the bigger market.

The exact rules behind each point (which levels it trusts, how it confirms an entry, the timeframes, the stop and target rules) are what I teach 1-on-1 and what the signals do for you.

Part 11

Your risk rules (write them down today)

Rules you decide when you're calm protect you from the decisions you'd make when you're not.

  • Risk per leverage trade: a fixed 0.5–1% of the account.
  • Daily loss limit: after 2–3 losses in a day, you stop until tomorrow.
  • Max trades per day: pick a number, and stop when you hit it.
  • Every trade has a stop before you enter. You never move it further away.
  • No trade without knowing the target and the R before you click.
  • No adding to a losing trade.
  • A losing week means smaller size next week, not bigger.
Part 12

Turning wins into cash flow: paying yourself

Trading profits only matter when they leave the exchange. Pick a split and run it every time a memecoin win or a profitable month comes in. For example:

Safe stack · 50%Stablecoins, Bitcoin or cash, off the exchange.
Pay yourself · 30%Real money to your bank. Rent, bills, life.
Back into trading · 20%Tops up the leverage account. Never more.

An example split, not advice. What matters is that you choose one and never break it to "win back" a loss.

  • Pay yourself on a schedule (weekly or monthly), not when you feel lucky.
  • Keep records of every trade and transfer. Trading profits are usually taxable; talk to a tax professional where you live.

Part 14

10 rules for your head

  1. A loss is a cost of business, not a judgment of you.
  2. Never trade to win back a loss. That trade is the one that blows accounts.
  3. FOMO is a signal you're late, not a reason to buy.
  4. If you're checking the chart every minute, your size is too big.
  5. Boring and profitable beats exciting and broke.
  6. Your best trade this month changes nothing. Your process does.
  7. "It'll come back" is how a small loss becomes a big one.
  8. Take breaks after a big win too. Overconfidence costs as much as fear.
  9. Don't compare your account to screenshots. Most of them don't show the losses.
  10. The goal isn't to be right. It's to make money when you're right and lose little when you're wrong.

Knowing the rules isn't the hard part. Following them is. In the mentorship I review your real trades with you every two weeks.
