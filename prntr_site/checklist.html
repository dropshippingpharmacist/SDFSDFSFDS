The 7-point checklist my bot runs before it risks a dollar

Most traders lose because they enter on a feeling. My bot can't. Every trade has to pass these seven checks, in this order. If one fails, it doesn't trade. It waits for the next one.

1

A real range, at least a day long

Price has to move sideways between a clear high and a clear low for at least 24 hours before the bot looks closer. A sharp V-shaped bounce doesn't count.

Why: a range is where big players quietly build their position. No range, no plan.

2

Three taps at the edge

Price has to touch the bottom of the range (or the top, for a short) three times. Each touch shows the other side running out of steam.

Why: two touches is a guess. Three is a pattern you can measure.

3

The stop hunt

The best setups have a tap that pokes just past the low and snaps back inside the range. That's everyone's stop losses getting taken out before the real move.

Why: once those stops are gone, there's fuel for price to go the other way. The bot wants to be on the side that took the liquidity, not the side that lost it.

4

A hidden order block underneath

The strongest taps land on a hidden order block: a spot on a higher timeframe where large orders were left behind and price moved away fast. Even better when it sits at the deep 0.705 to 0.786 pullback of the last move.

Why: those leftover orders defend the level. You're buying where the big money is still waiting to buy.

5

A break of structure, on a candle close

Nothing happens until a candle closes beyond the last swing inside the range. A wick through it doesn't count. Then the bot zooms in to the 3-minute chart for the exact trigger.

Why: the close is proof the other side gave up. Entering before it is guessing.

6

At least 3 to 1

The stop goes just past the third tap. The first target is the other side of the range, and the runner rides to where the most traders get force-closed. If the trade can't pay at least three times what it risks, it's skipped.

Why: at 3 to 1 you can be wrong more often than you're right and still grow the account.

7

Still at the entry, and the market agrees

The trade is only taken while price is still at the entry. If it already ran, the bot lets it go. For altcoins, Bitcoin has to be setting up the same way. Every trade risks the same fixed amount.

Why: chasing a move that already happened is how good setups turn into bad trades.

What the bot always skips

  • No clear range, or a range younger than a day
  • A wick through the level with no candle close
  • Anything that pays less than 3 to 1
  • A move that already left the entry
  • Altcoins fighting the direction of Bitcoin
