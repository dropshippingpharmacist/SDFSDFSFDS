## 1. How We Scaled This Trader From $8k/m To $1.2M/month (selling trading courses)

This is the master case study (about 2.5 hours). The client went from $8k/mo to $1.23M/mo in 4 months, all organic.

**METRICS (headline)**
- Starting point: $8k/mo from a $200/mo low-ticket Discord, 1 YouTube video, 300 YT subs, about 1,000 IG followers, no niche. He had $1.6M in verified prop-firm payouts but wasn't using them.
- Path: $8k → about $100k+ in month 1 (consulting) → $752k in June → $1.23M in July. More than $2.8M in total since joining. On track for about $1.4–1.6M in August.
- July: $1.23M cash, $1.5M revenue, about 80% cash-to-revenue. One-day high of $65k.
- Processor split: about $959k through Whop (caption says "W/wo"; most volume moved there for better financing) plus $168k through Fansbase (caption says "comments/fan bases"), plus about $100k through wire, Wise and crypto, which can't be tracked.
- About 90% of leads and revenue come from YouTube (about $900k/mo); the rest from IG and other sources.
- Channel size: 20–22k YT subs and about 20k IG followers produce more than $1M/mo. "You don't need to go viral."
- $0 ad spend for the whole scale-up. Ads started only in early August.
- Organic tracking (Hyros, caption "Tracko/track your"): $1.4M cash tracked to organic content in the last 30 days. One YouTube video is attributed $900k. Others: $100k and $200k.
- Old offer economics: $200/mo with about 2-month average retention, so LTV about $400 and AOV $200.
- 3 story sequences produced $40k/day back-to-back-to-back (about $120k), and about $200k in that week. Hanck's own story sequences: "$300k last month in pure profit."
- After the offer switch: 0 disputes, 0 refunds, 0 chargebacks. Payment-plan completion is above 95%, against an industry average of about 60–70%.
- Selling solo before the sales team: $28k revenue and $22k cash in the first week.
- Sales team on JJ: 12+ reps (6 setters, 6 closers) plus a sales manager. More than 30 sales calls a day. About 2,500–3,000 calls in the AI bot's database.
- The one-on-one upsell alone adds $300–400k/mo for JJ.
- Hanck's own agency plus consulting revenue: more than $350k/mo.

**CONTENT: foundations (do these first)**
- Four foundations: (1) niche, (2) USP, (3) ICP / target audience, (4) being yourself on camera. A fake persona leads to burnout.
- Niche: prop-firm trading. It's "aspirational, constantly searched, easy to understand."
- USP examples: AlexG's is "set and forget," which "subconsciously clowns on scalpers" and ties to swing trading. JJ's is results-based: tax reports, verified payouts, a quant/data-driven background, and $1.6M in verified payouts. The USP is also built into the VSL.
- ICP: aim at older, intermediate-to-advanced, financially established, skeptical or "burned before" traders who are drawn to data, not lifestyle. Pre-handle the "burned before" objection in the VSL.
- Why older: highest PIF rate, highest cash-to-revenue, and the highest show and close rates. Young people "don't have enough pain" (they haven't been unprofitable for years) and "don't have any money."
- JJ's YouTube has zero lifestyle content apart from one vlog, which is itself educational (flying out students and trading live).

**CONTENT: YouTube engine**
- Use a top / middle / bottom-of-funnel (TOF/MOF/BOF) mix. TOF is broad and gets views and leads but converts poorly. MOF is more niche and nurtures. BOF is the most niche and advanced, and converts. "You need all three." The exact percentages are kept for the paid program.
- TOF title examples: "The exact trading strategy that made me $1.2M in the last 12 months," "I made $1.5M trading and learned this" (about 100k views), "This one candle makes me $100K per month." Broad hooks are filtered through the USP, and everything shown is verified.
- Typical buying journey: TOF → binge MOF → BOF → book a call.
- The algorithm rewards click-through rate (thumbnail plus title) and average view duration, because YouTube makes money by serving ads on the best videos.
- Hook = first 5–10 seconds, both visual (what you show) and verbal (what you say).
- Retention: switch between chart breakdowns, live annotations, trade recaps and live trading.
- Reward/CTA by funnel stage: TOF → freebie or free community (email opt-in, then email nurture); MOF → soft mention of the mentorship; BOF → hard pitch. BOF examples: backtesting, spreadsheet stuff, YouTube testimonials. "Weird" to hard-pitch on TOF because viewers haven't seen your face. A typical path: about 2 TOF + 2 MOF + 1 BOF videos, then they convert.
- Session time is a separate metric from average view duration (how many videos someone watches before leaving). Documentary series raise session time.

**CONTENT: Instagram**
- Reels are educational. Any lifestyle reel must still contain numbers. Topics: quant, strategy, payouts, results.
- Built a short-form series, "documenting the journey to another $1M in payouts": day-in-the-life clips of 20–40 seconds with trade recaps, breakdowns and payouts.
- The documentary runs daily on short-form and weekly on YouTube ("episode 1–4," a playlist, "second million in prop-firm payouts"). It's treated as MOF. A viewer who lands on day 67 scrolls back to day 1 and binges, which creates FOMO.
- Story sequences follow the same hook → retention → reward structure. Example hook: "-$50k in trading… now making $750,000+ in payouts." The middle shows student journey testimonials. Close with a "low pressure DM or apply CTA."
- Save story sequences to Highlights so they become permanent assets, like YouTube videos. Reels are "forgotten after a week," so don't spend all your time on short-form.
- Hanck documents client months day by day in stories, highlights and reels ("Day 2 of scaling a trading client to $3M total").
- Pinned reels (3): (1) a strategy breakdown with numbers that builds FOMO and sends people to YouTube, not DMs; (2) day 1, 2 or 3 of the documentary series; (3) pure proof: payouts, tax reports, business bank accounts. Together these are enough for a skeptical cold lead.
- Use the IG Notes feature at the top of DMs as well.
- Flywheel: IG builds trust through daily proof and your face; YouTube builds authority and sells. Send IG traffic to YouTube, and vice versa.
- TikTok and X aren't needed. At most, repost reels to TikTok or Facebook for extra traffic.
- A free Discord or community is a nurturing mechanism only. "Not to give away the full strategy for free." Templates are provided to clients.

**FUNNEL**
- ManyChat: keyword DM automations. Trick: an automated "personalized" voice note sent on a story reply or DM, with a 1–2 minute delay so it doesn't look automated. After that, "DM setter goes crazy."
- No checkout links. Sell the next click: content → application → booking → post-booking → call.
- Booking form (iClosed or GHL): name, phone, what they're struggling with (pain points the setter uses when dialling), and whether they're a fit (financial qualification, seriousness).
- Auto-disqualify: under $1k to invest goes to a separate sequence, where the sales team phones them to try to close the downsell.
- VSL page structure: header → guarantee / risk reversal → FOMO and scarcity → booking form → testimonials and screenshots → booking form again → more payout proof → more.
- Always split-test funnels. Two funnels are live, with different headlines, risk reversals and brand colours but the same VSL.
- Two VSLs: (1) a schedule-call VSL that sells the booking and creates urgency to book now; (2) a post-booking VSL that sells showing up (urgency, scarcity, limited), backed by a presentation template, reminders, "what to do" and an FAQ.
- Freebie funnel: landing page (headline, image, opt-in, plus a booking option) → thank-you page with a waitlist option → email via Kit.
- Email sequences by scenario: freebie lead; applicant who hasn't booked; closed (onboarding and welcome); no-close; no-show.
- SMS: post-booking reminders, pre-call reminders, SMS blasts, offer and freebie opt-ins.
- Email/SMS sets for pre-launch, post-launch, evergreen, freebie-specific and waitlist.
- Verify your sending domain before emailing. Don't send email through GHL, because "it lands in spam." Use Kit.

**CONTENT: copywriting**
- Two levers: pain (name their exact situation) and curiosity (hint at the solution).
- Golden rule: "never sell the program, sell the next click."
- Marketing angles: seasonal (e.g. "volatility during summer months"), New Year's ("didn't become profitable in all of 2025/2026"), launch angles, quarterly, specific pain angles (overtrading, over-leverage), social proof.
- Rotate copy between three types: testimonial, case study and breakdown. Never run two hard pitches back to back.
- VSL scripting hack: copy a top VSL video's transcript (via VidIQ) → paste into Claude with the VSL framework template → tailor it to your brand voice. Keep a human review loop and feed in the offer, voice and angle.
- Discord announcements need copy too: anticipation teases, hook → proof → CTA, direct CTA posts, wins drops, urgency posts.

**OFFER**
- Old offer: a $200/mo Discord with live trading, no structure, no guarantee, no contracts, high churn. "Low price = low perceived value" (the $5 vs $1,000 shoes analogy). He says it brings beginners, disputes, chargebacks and refunds, and calls low-ticket "the definition of headache."
- New offer suite:
  - Main: group coaching at $6.5k, 6 months.
  - Upsell: premium one-on-one at $35–40k, 3–4 months.
  - Downsell: $2k (still four figures; can run 6, 9 or 12 months).
  - No low-ticket or mid-ticket offers.
- Pitch order: the main offer is pitched about 90–93% of the time. Pitch the $40k one-on-one only when a prospect is very qualified. Payment ladder: (1) card pay-in-full → (2) financing pay-in-full (you get cash upfront, they split it) → (3) internal payment plan → (4) downsell → (5) deposit to join later. Never lead with the downsell.
- Guarantee: conditional. "If you don't get at least one payout within the program duration, full refund," provided they do X, Y and Z. It's written into the contract.
- Duration: about 6 months for group ("sweet spot"), 3–4 months for one-on-one, 6–12 months for the downsell. Never lifetime, because it kills urgency and results.
- Phase unlocks to protect payment-plan completion. Example: a $6k program paid $2k upfront, $2k at day 30 and $2k at day 60. Phase 1 = foundations, psychology, strategy. Phase 2 (after the 2nd payment) = getting payouts, passing prop firms. Phase 3 (after the 3rd) = scaling prop firms, copy trading. This also prevents overwhelm.
- What group coaching includes: group coaching calls (the backbone, all recorded so you answer once instead of 50 times in DMs), a private community for sharing wins, a course outline, resources, docs and student roadmaps.
- Group coaching leaves out one-on-one access and direct support from JJ, so the upsell still has a reason to exist.
- One-on-one includes: scheduled calls, 24/7 DM access, a personalized roadmap and feedback, live trading-journal reviews, a fixed duration, plus everything in group. Set clear expectations; no unlimited call time.
- Price the one-on-one from your hourly rate. At $1M/mo and 100 hours you earn about $10k/hr, so "$5k across 4 months" one-on-one isn't worth it. Hence $35–40k.
- One-on-one sales logic: students can make "at least $50k/mo trading prop firms," so a $35–40k investment is simple ROI maths. (Caption arithmetic: "30k × 12 = $36,000," which is garbled.)
- When to upsell one-on-one to group students: right after a win (payout, funded, breakthrough), when they ask, or when they hit a wall group can't solve.
- Maximize LTV through downsell → main → one-on-one upsells plus renewals.
- Run one-on-one first up to about $30–60k/mo, then switch to group as the main offer. JJ closed 5 one-on-ones (about $20k) in his first 3–4 days, then went group.
- Financing: the provider pays you upfront minus 5–15% fees; the student splits it over 3, 6, 12 or 24 months. Having processed eight figures gives clients "first priority" financing and lower fees with Fansbase and Whop. Selling high ticket without financing means "missing out on too much money."

**DELIVERY & RETENTION**
- Every offer (main, upsell, downsell) has a contract covering scope, what's included and excluded, and the guarantee with its qualifying conditions. No program access until the contract is signed. Contracts are stored.
- Automated onboarding: payment → Zapier triggers the contract (DocuSign) → once signed, a welcome email sequence → Typeform market-research form → program access.
- Market-research form after purchase asks: which platform, which specific YouTube video, the tipping point, how long the buying journey was, what almost stopped them. Double down on the videos that convert people. Hanck uses this for his own offer too.
- Share student wins from the community into story highlights, testimonials and BOF videos.
- Referral program, tracked.
- Failed-payment Zap: a card decline on a call or a failed plan renewal pings the team, who follow up "same minute." Causes: expired card, new card, bank flag, limit hit. "One of the quietest ways a coaching business bleeds money."
- Financial tracking: expenses (editors, designers), profit margins, an LTV tracker, payment-plan completion, dispute and refund rates. Hire a CSM (client success manager) at scale.
- He attributes zero disputes to on-point content, a real offer, reps who don't oversell, prospects knowing what they're buying, and a conditional guarantee.
- Client trips as incentives: fly top-performing clients to Dubai, Miami or a 10-bedroom Croatia villa. Record in-person testimonials and document the trip. Got JJ onto the Titans of Tomorrow podcast for brand awareness.

**TEAM & OPS: tool stack (13 for JJ; most people need a third to a half)**
- GoHighLevel (CRM, funnels, automations, pipelines).
- Close (sales CRM; "only needed at multi-6 figures").
- iClosed (booking).
- Wistia (VSL hosting and tracking: play rate, conversion, retention, CTR, engagement).
- Kit (email).
- Typeform (market research).

- Zapier ("the glue").
- n8n (caption "NAD"; a more advanced Zapier, for some onboarding).

- Fathom (AI call recording).
- Hyros (organic content attribution).
- Looker Studio (dashboards).
- Plus Whop and Fansbase for payments, ManyChat, and VidIQ.
- Wistia diagnostic: low play rate but high retention means the VSL is good and the header or presentation is the problem.
- GHL pipelines: dragging a lead to "Closed" triggers the contract, and signing triggers onboarding.
- Zaps into Discord channels:
  - Applications that didn't book (name and phone, so setters dial).
  - Meetings booked (goals, struggles, investment level, assigned rep, email, IG, phone). Setters reach out by IG or WhatsApp on the client's behalf and dial before the call to confirm the show.
  - Setter threads per lead (auto-delete after 7–14 days) with context such as "works a 9–5."
  - Cancelled calls, with reason (budget/financial DQ).
  - Payments (amount, method, financing split). Reps "spam emojis."
  - Failed payments.
  - End-of-call reports (notes, Fathom link, source, setter, closer, date).
  - End-of-day reports (per setter, closer and manager: calls, productivity, revenue).
- Custom AI Discord bot:
  - Every Fathom recording goes to the bot, which scores the call on discovery and pitch against more than 2,500 previous calls. It pulls pain points, goals, reasons for buying and hesitancy, plus the top 3 pain points and takeaways.
  - The manager only reviews calls rated 1 out of 5 stars.
  - It flags common objections. Example: "prospects not warm enough" means make more MOF and BOF content. Hanck calls this "an infinite feedback loop."
  - It generates custom payment links 24/7 (e.g. $4,975, any platform or financing) so reps never wait for a manager.
  - It tracks the follow-up / collection list ("$300,000 to collect"), with next due dates.
- Looker dashboard KPIs: cash collected, revenue, cash-to-revenue, show rate, offer rate, PIF rate, pitch-to-close, LTV (separate board), meetings, shows, pitched, closed, AOV, cash per call.
- How he reads the dashboard: AOV down probably means content is attracting younger people. Conversion down probably means a funnel or copy problem.
- Sales-team board: closers (calls due, taken, closed, close rate, cash, commission) and setters (set, showed, closed, show rate, close rate, cash, commission). Commissions are calculated from it. "Who to bonus, who to fire."
- Rule: "Nothing manual in your business, even at $10–20k/mo." Everything except content should be automated or delegated. Reverse-engineer everything from the target, e.g. "can this scale to $100k/mo without breaking?"
- Faces beat faceless brands, especially in a "trust recession."

**SALES**
- Below $10–20k/mo, don't hire a sales team. Learn sales yourself: setting, discovery, pre-handling, pitching, closing, objection handling, deposits, follow-ups. Otherwise "you can't tell a closer if he's doing a good job."
- JJ self-set and self-closed $22k in a week, then the in-house team was added.
- Commission norms: closer about 10%, setter about 5% "on average in this industry." At $10k/mo a closer makes $1k, so good reps won't join. At $20–40k/mo it starts to work. "No good sales rep would ever work for an offer doing 10–15K/mo; at minimum 20K."
- Roles: DM setters, phone setters (dialers), closers, a sales manager. The owners manage the manager. The manager vets, hires, trains, reviews and coaches calls, and replaces reps who get "comfortable."
- Reps came from big trading offers: Q Banks (unclear), Mamba FX, AlexG, TDR.
- Setters and closers need different training, management and coaching calls ("different egos").
- Incentives: weekly, bi-weekly, monthly and quarterly bonuses; custom plaques (e.g. $1M collected, which is about $100k in commission for a closer); team trips to Dubai; dinners; birthday shout-outs. "Team culture is the number one priority" once you have talent.
- Leaderboards: setter-plus-closer duos (from his Fortnite pro background) compete as teams against other duos on real-time cash and revenue.
- Metrics the sales manager tracks:
  - Applicant quality, dials, calls, DMs.
  - Connection rate (dial → connect → book), total calls booked.
  - SDR bookings vs auto-bookings, and show rate for each.
  - DQ rate, close rate, AOV, cash collected per call.

**Agency vs consulting (business model)**
- Below $100k/mo, you don't need a done-for-you agency. A 10–25% rev share on $10k/mo is about $2k, so no talented agency will take you.
- If you outsource everything from $0 you learn nothing, and "if the agency leaves, your business crashes."
- Path: done-with-you consulting first (with some done-for-you backend and sales) until you pass $100k/mo, then the DFY agency on rev share. Hanck's agency won't take clients below roughly $200–400k/mo unless they can reach $1M/mo within 3 months.
- Order he uses to build: content → offer → operations/backend → mentorship/fulfilment → copy → sales → launch.
- Highest levers: (1) content, (2) operations, (3) sales. The mentorship is "glue." The launch is "a bonus."

**FUNNEL: launch**
- Launch when switching low → high ticket ("99.9% of the time"). Otherwise do an evergreen launch.
- Pre-launch: a day-by-day content calendar of Discord announcements, emails, SMS, story sequences and maybe YouTube videos. Stack proof during the window and tailor it to an angle (New Year's, low-ticket → one-on-one, group, summer/seasonal).
- Before launching: offer locked, marketing on point, funnels and copy tested, audience warmed.
- The warmest followers buy first when contacted directly (DMs, stories, emails, Discord, SMS blasts).

**MISTAKES / DON'TS**
- Low-ticket subscription Discords ($50–200/mo).
- Lifestyle content aimed at young people.
- Running ads before organic works: "ads are fuel to the fire; no fire, no point." Ads only from about six figures or multi-six figures.
- Hard-pitching on TOF.
- Lifetime access.
- Giving the full strategy away free in Discord.
- Manual contracts, access granting or checkout links.
- Sending email from GHL.
- Hiring a sales team below $20k/mo.
- Hiring a DFY agency below $100k/mo.
- Copying someone else's funnel without tailoring it to your USP.
- Pricing one-on-one below your hourly value.
- Leaving failed payments unattended.
- Not tracking ("you don't know what you don't know").

## 2. The Secret To Making Viral Trading Content (step-by-step)

He breaks down his old trading video, which got 130k+ views and made more than $50k.

**CONTENT**
- Formula for everything (long-form, short-form, story sequences, emails, ads): hook → retention → reward.
- Hook window: short-form is the first 1–2 seconds; long-form is the first 5–15 seconds.
- Platform metrics: long-form is click-through rate (thumbnail plus title) and average view duration. Short-form is hook rate (stopping the scroll) and average view duration. Platforms push what keeps people watching ads.
- Long-form makes more money than short-form for trading education.
- Visual hook: show the journey (pictures, "staying up at 2am / 4am watching trading YouTube videos 5 years ago"). Verbal hook: pain points, bold claims, big promises, a relatable story. Use both in the same 5–15 seconds.
- His hook script (paraphrased): "3 years of constantly blowing accounts, 3 years of feeling stuck, 3 years of binge-watching YouTube 10/12/14 hours a day…" He's deliberately relatable about strategy hopping, every indicator and over-leveraging.
- High-converting hook = pain points → proof (proof of concept, payouts, legitimacy) → promise ("watch till the end and you get my strategy that got me profitable").
- Retention tip 1: switch format at least every 3–4 minutes. His video ran: about minute 0–1 pain points; 1–4 theory with drawn candlesticks; 4–7 line chart; 7–10 candlesticks; then trade breakdowns; then recorded live trading ("made 1k or 2k"); then live breakdowns #1–3. Never sit on TradingView talking for 10 minutes.
- Retention tip 2: movement. Draw or annotate every 5–10 seconds, circle and highlight, zoom in and out, keep the screen dynamic (don't overdo it).
- Retention tip 3: show proof (screenshots, losses, results, live trades). "Visually and verbally = maximum retention."
- Retention tip 4: energy and delivery. Match energy to the content, don't over-hype or scream, be authentic. Stutters are fine because "it's called a personal brand."
- Reward: value (a strategy), or a freebie (PDF, checklist, journal), or a pitch. His freebie was a 16-page PDF of his strategy (text plus screenshots), delivered inside the free Discord after an email opt-in.

**FUNNEL**
- CTA by funnel stage: TOF broad videos plug the freebie or free Discord ("never say buy my mentorship at the end"). MOF videos make soft mentorship mentions. BOF videos (very in-depth and niche, e.g. "how to actually beat the mental game of trading") pitch the mentorship directly.
- Nurture benchmark ("a Google study," per Hanck): a lead needs about 4 platforms, 7 touchpoints and 11 interactions. Example: 2 YouTube videos, 3 Discord pings, 5 IG reels, story sequences, 2 emails. Outliers range from 1 day to 3 months, and some buy off one video.
- Post-purchase market-research form: which video was the tipping point, what resonated, which platform. Use the answers to improve content.

**METRICS**
- 130k+ views and more than $50k from one video.
- Freebie: 16 pages.
- 4 platforms / 7 touchpoints / 11 interactions.

**MISTAKES / DON'TS**
- Being boring and static. Spending more than 3–4 minutes in one format. Hard-pitching on TOF videos (it's "not trustworthy," since it's their first video). Screaming or over-hyping.

## 4. How I Make $30k/week Selling Trading Courses

This one covers the content ecosystem he uses for himself and his clients.

**METRICS**
- About $33k in 6 days. Days of $7.5k, $15k, $15.5k (15.2 after fees), $5.6k and $3k. With 3 calls left he expected $35–40k for the week, on pace for $100k that month.
- His own trading brand went from $0 to more than $50k/mo in under 4 months using only IG and YouTube.
- Client results he shows: "Crabs" $2k → $25k/mo, someone $0 → $33k, "Nico/Beo" (unclear) $700 → $25k.
- $33k/week came from a YouTube channel of 600 subscribers, because they're his exact ICP (traders who have a brand or want to start one and scale it).

**CONTENT**
- Short-form (IG) is for awareness. Long-form (YouTube) is for nurture and conversion.
- TikTok and X are only for extra traffic: "shouldn't be something you sell on."
- IG: don't post only educational/BOF content; mix it up. Include "brain rot" awareness posts (e.g. a 28k-view reel about AlexG and Bugattis) to get follows and face recognition, plus in-depth educational reels for nurture.
- YouTube: only educational long-form videos of 14–23 minutes. No YouTube Shorts.
- IG highlights: case studies, client testimonials and Q&A. These redirect to YouTube rather than selling directly. If people are ready, they DM.
- When cold leads DM you, send them to YouTube videos first: "they've never seen you before, they won't buy."
- Cover every pain point with a matching video, e.g. one for selling low ticket and one for not scaling.

**FUNNEL (ecosystem / flywheel)**
- IG → YouTube → VSL → book → post-booking VSL → email confirmation → appointment reminders (WhatsApp, SMS, email automations, which link back to YouTube case studies and IG) → call → close.
- Leads who disqualify are sent to a mid-ticket VSL "to milk out as much money as humanly possible." Separate automated sequences exist for qualified, disqualified and post-booking leads.
- Ads funnel (only at scale, after organic): Meta ad → opt-in for a "blueprint" → YouTube → email sequence → YouTube, IG, VSL. The first emails don't push the VSL; they nurture first.
- Full journey: cold → IG (sees your face via short-form) → YouTube nurture → free Discord → freebie opt-in (email captured) → email sequences hit every pain point → VSL → book → close (by you, or by a sales team "if you have the traffic for it").

## Cross-video patterns in this batch

- **Hook → retention → reward** is his universal formula for YouTube, reels, story sequences, emails, ads and Discord announcements. Hook windows are 1–2 seconds for short-form and 5–15 seconds for long-form. Switch format every 3–4 minutes or less, and draw or annotate every 5–10 seconds.
- **Platforms have separate jobs.** IG short-form is for awareness and daily trust; YouTube long-form is for authority and conversion (about 90% of revenue for high ticket). TikTok and X are optional. Every IG touchpoint and cold DM goes to YouTube. A tiny ICP-tight channel can print: 600 subs gave $33k/week, and 20k subs gave more than $1M/mo.
- **CTA depends on funnel stage.** TOF gets a freebie or free Discord, MOF a soft mentorship mention, BOF a hard pitch. Leads need about 4 platforms, 7 touchpoints and 11 interactions.
- **Sell the next click, not the program.** Content → VSL → application/booking (with qualification and auto-DQ under $1k) → post-booking VSL → reminders (SMS, WhatsApp, email) → call. No checkout links. Disqualified leads go to a downsell or mid-ticket path.
- **High ticket only.** No $50–200/mo Discords. Structure: a $6.5k, 6-month group main offer, a $35–40k one-on-one upsell, a $2k downsell, a conditional "1 payout or refund" guarantee, contracts, no lifetime access, and phase unlocks tied to payment-plan installments. The pitch ladder runs PIF card → financing → payment plan → downsell → deposit.
- **Target older, intermediate/advanced, pained, financially able traders** with data and proof content, not lifestyle. He ties this to higher PIF rate, show rate, close rate and cash-to-revenue.
- **Organic first, ads later.** Ads come only at six to multi-six figures ("fuel to the fire"). JJ reached $1.23M/mo on $0 ad spend. Hanck now spends $15–20k/mo on Meta.
- **Track everything and close the loop.** He uses Looker dashboards, Hyros revenue per video, Wistia VSL metrics, end-of-call reports with Fathom, and an AI bot that scores calls and mines objections. Objections become MOF/BOF content, and post-purchase market-research forms show which video triggered the sale.
- **Sales team only after about $20k/mo.** Before that the founder learns to sell (JJ did $22k in a week solo). Closers get about 10% and setters about 5%. Structure: DM setters, phone setters, closers, a manager, then the owners. Bonuses, plaques, trips and duo leaderboards keep reps competitive.
- **Automate everything except content.** GHL, iClosed, Zapier, DocuSign, Kit and Typeform run onboarding. Zaps in Discord cover applications, bookings, payments, failed payments (same-minute recovery) and end-of-call/end-of-day reports.
- **Documenting progress day by day** (story sequences saved to highlights, documentary reels and YouTube series, client trips) is his main FOMO and conversion asset, and doubles as testimonial content. Benchmarks: 3 sequences gave $120k; a 30-day launch hit 83% show rate and 74–77% close rate.
- **Business model shift.** He moved from DFY management on rev share for everyone to done-with-you consulting or a DFY build-out first, then an agency only past $100k/mo. He now runs multiple offers by client stage and raises prices transparently.
